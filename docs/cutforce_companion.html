1What Cut Force measures

A receiver who changes direction pushes sideways against the ground. That push is centripetal acceleration, which the tracking data gives frame by frame as speed times turn rate.

Lateral acceleration, each frame

$$a_{\perp}(t) = v(t)\,\left|\frac{d\theta}{dt}\right|$$

\(v\) is speed in yards per second and \(\theta\) is the direction of travel in radians, unwrapped and smoothed with a three-frame rolling mean at 10 Hz. Frames below 3 yd/s are excluded.

Cut Force, one rep

$$\mathrm{CF} = \frac{1}{g}\cdot\frac{1}{3}\sum_{i \in \text{top 3 of } [k-3,\,k+3]} a_{\perp}(i), \quad k = \arg\max_t a_{\perp}(t)$$

The mean of the three highest frames around the peak, in g (\(g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2} = 10.72\ \mathrm{yd/s^2}\)).

Standardized within drill and class

$$z_{ij} = \frac{\mathrm{CF}_{ij} - \bar{\mathrm{CF}}_{d(j),\,c(i)}}{s_{d(j),\,c(i)}}$$

Each rep \(j\) of player \(i\) is scored against the same drill \(d\) in the same draft class \(c\), because the 2025 export differs from earlier years.

Empirical Bayes player score

$$\hat{c}_i = \frac{\tau^2}{\tau^2 + \sigma^2 / n_i}\,\bar{z}_i$$

A player with three reps is pulled toward the mean more than a player with twelve. \(\tau^2\) is the between-player variance and \(\sigma^2\) the within-player rep variance.

Split-half reliability

$$\rho_{SB} = \frac{2r_{hh}}{1 + r_{hh}}$$

Drills are split into two random halves 500 times, the halves are correlated, and the Spearman-Brown correction scales to the full set. The median is reported.

Attenuation-corrected transfer

$$r_{\text{true}} = \frac{r_{xy}}{\sqrt{\rho_{xx}\,\rho_{yy}}}$$ $$= \frac{0.23}{\sqrt{0.59 \times 0.43}} \approx 0.45$$

An upper bound. It assumes classical independent error in both measures, so the raw 0.23 is the headline.

Targets model

$$\Pr(\text{target}_{r}) = \sigma\!\left(\alpha_{i} + \beta\,T_i + \gamma^{\top} x_{r}\right), \quad \alpha_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_\alpha^2)$$

Bayesian hierarchical logit in PyMC on 34,889 routes. \(x_r\) holds route type, alignment, coverage, down, draft slot, and weight. Combine Cut Force enters as a noisy measure of the latent trait \(T_i\) with reliability 0.59.

Method

Game Cut Force uses the same formula from the snap to the throw on out, in, slant, post, corner, cross, and hitch routes, then residualizes on route type and entry speed before the player mean is shrunk. Odd and even route reliability of the game score is 0.43.

4Sharper cuts, more targets

Player-level view of the result: targets per route run against Combine Cut Force for receivers with at least 100 regular-season routes. The brief's headline number comes from the route-level hierarchical model, which also controls for route, alignment, coverage, down, draft slot, and weight.

Figure 4. r = , n = . Gold squares are quintile means. The hierarchical model gives +1.9 points per SD (95% interval 0.5 to 3.4, P(>0) = 0.99), a conditional association rather than a causal effect. Without Puka Nacua the effect is +1.0 (interval −0.2 to 2.3).

Method

Targets per route run is targets divided by routes. The quintile means use the standardized, shrunk Combine score. The route-level model is in Section 1; the clustered logit check gives an odds ratio of 1.13 per SD (p = 0.062).

5Brakes, cut, or burst

Every rep and route split into phases around the same peak. Only the cut repeats across Combine drills and carries over to games.

Figure 5. Four phases on the same checks. Darker is better within a column. The right column is the odds ratio for targets per route in a joint player-clustered logit with all four phases: Cut Force 1.15 (p = 0.016), deceleration 0.87, acceleration 1.03, speed kept 0.98.
Method and equations
$$\text{decel} = \max\!\big(0, -\min_{t \in [k-10,\,k]} \dot v(t)\big)/g, \qquad \text{accel} = \max\!\big(0, \max_{t \in [k,\,k+10]} \dot v(t)\big)/g, \qquad \text{kept} = \frac{\min_{t\in[k-3,k+3]} v(t)}{\max_{t\in[k-8,k]} v(t)}$$

Deceleration is the largest frame-level slowing in the second before the peak, acceleration the largest speeding up in the second after, and speed kept the lowest speed at the break over the entry speed.

6Ten ways to measure the break

The first-draft feedback asked for alternative break measurements. All ten definitions below run on the same 967 Combine reps and are scored on the same checks.

Figure 6. Darker is better within a column. The peak definitions correlate with the primary at the player level (r = 0.94 to 0.99). The first-break-only definition is the strongest alternative. The mean across the turn scores close to the peak, while the integrated load and the turn's duration are much weaker, likely because they reward long rounded turns as much as sharp ones. Every number is produced by Section 15 of the Kaggle notebook on regular-season routes: transfer uses the 82 receivers with breaking routes, the target and held-out columns the 62 receivers with at least 100 routes.
Equations for the alternatives
$$\text{first break: } k_1 = \min\{t : a_\perp(t) \ge 0.6\,\max a_\perp,\ a_\perp(t) \text{ is a local peak}\}$$ $$\text{integrated: } I = \frac{1}{g\,f}\sum_{t \in T} a_\perp(t), \qquad \text{mean: } \bar{a} = \frac{1}{g\,|T|}\sum_{t\in T} a_\perp(t), \qquad T = \{t : a_\perp(t) \ge \tfrac{1}{2} a_\perp(k)\} \text{ contiguous around } k$$

\(f = 10\) Hz. Single peak frame drops the three-frame average. Five-frame smoothing widens the rolling mean on speed and heading from three frames to five.

7Does it predict a new draft class?

The second piece of feedback asked for validation on a held-out draft class. A player-level model of targets per route (draft slot, slot share, weight) is fit on two classes and predicts the third. The question is whether Cut Force explains what that model missed.

Figure 7. Correlation between Cut Force and the held-out residual target rate, by held-out class. The signal is clear in 2023 (three NFL seasons) and absent in 2024 and 2025. The primary definition does not lower held-out RMSE (pooled 4.59 base vs 4.67). First break lowers it to 4.51, and all of that gain sits in the 2023 fold. Cut Force is a measured trait, not yet a draft-day predictor.
Method and equations
$$\widehat{\mathrm{TPRR}}_i^{(-c)} = \hat\beta_0 + \hat\beta_1 \log(\text{pick}_i) + \hat\beta_2\,\text{slot}_i + \hat\beta_3\,\text{wt}_i \quad \text{fit on classes } \ne c$$ $$e_i = \mathrm{TPRR}_i - \widehat{\mathrm{TPRR}}_i^{(-c)}, \qquad r_c = \mathrm{corr}(\hat c_i, e_i)\ \text{for } i \in c, \qquad \mathrm{RMSE}_c = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{n_c}\sum_{i\in c} e_i^2}$$

Receivers with at least 100 regular-season routes, n = 28, 19, and 15 by class. The route-level version in the Kaggle notebook (leave one class out, log loss 0.4507 base vs 0.4508 with Cut Force) reaches the same conclusion.

8Does it describe the defender?

The same metric on 118 defensive backs across six Combine movement drills and 30,602 coverage snaps. It does not hold, and the 40 still predicts man-coverage separation.

Figure 8A. Receivers and defensive backs on the same three checks.
Figure 8B. Combine 40 time against mean separation at the throw in man coverage, DBs with at least 15 matched snaps. r = , n = . In the snap-level model each extra 0.1 s in the 40 adds 0.28 yards of separation (p = 0.006), while DB Cut Force adds nothing (p = 0.41).
Method

Each rookie DB in man coverage is paired with the nearest rookie receiver at the snap (within 12 yards). Pairs within 10 yards at the throw are kept (2,329 snaps). Separation at the throw is modeled with OLS, route and snap distance as controls, standard errors clustered by DB. The reliability failure does not depend on the pairing rule.
